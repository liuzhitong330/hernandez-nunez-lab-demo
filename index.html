The lab asks how neural circuits sense and control the body. Its heart–brain work identifies a cholinergic hindbrain population involved in cardiac inhibition. To connect physiology to molecular identity, a practical first question is which additional markers could narrow a broad cholinergic population into a tractable spatial validation panel.

I built a small tool over the public WARP zebrafish reference dataset to compare chata co-markers, one fish at a time. It asks what is gained in anatomical concentration, what is lost in coverage, and whether a promising combination survives an animal held out from selection. These are candidates for validation, not identified cardiac neurons.

How it works

WARP co-maps molecular expression and neural activity. Here I use its author-classified expression calls and atlas labels: 274,455 spatially mapped cells from three fish, with 41 assayed genes. The target below is an anatomical search area, not a functional cell type. For each chata + co-marker intersection, concentration is the fraction in that area; coverage is the fraction of that area's chata-positive cells retained.

Analysis 2 — Does the choice survive another fish?

Decision
Rank the weakest animal, not the pooled average

Among co-markers meeting the coverage and cell-count floor in every fish, rank by the lowest per-fish anatomical concentration. Change the floors to see when a narrower panel becomes too sparse. A tie uses the gene name, not an undisclosed score.

Why this matters for the lab

This analysis is aligned with the role's spatial transcriptomics, molecular profiling and transgenic zebrafish work. It separates an inexpensive first decision—what to co-label—from the harder decision of how to target a circuit. A useful pilot would measure chata, a shortlisted co-marker and anatomical landmarks in independent larvae, count double-positive cells inside and outside the physiological region of interest, and retain single-marker and negative-probe controls.

Only after spatial and functional validation would it make sense to assess an enhancer or transgenic reporter. Endogenous co-expression does not guarantee a faithful driver; a reporter needs its own sensitivity and off-target validation. To establish cardiac relevance, the new molecularly defined population would also need simultaneous heart-rate/neuronal measurements and controlled perturbation. None of those experiments is claimed here.

Methods note

This uses an external reference atlas, not data from the Hernandez-Nunez Lab. Four original arrays contain 306,108 gene-positive cells. Excluding 31,653 rows with non-finite spatial coordinates leaves 274,455. The supplied binary calls are intersected with chata; no re-normalization or new spot-count cutoff is introduced. Regional labels and the 41-gene order follow the documented Janelia reference viewer mapping. “Medulla” pools its inferior, intermediate and superior labels; this is not a segmentation of the paper's medial premotor population.

The WARP reference is larval zebrafish (the source documents 6 dpf imaging), not a developmental replication of the heart–brain study. Sampling, registration, detection sensitivity and preselection for gene-positive cells limit the denominators. The 41-gene panel is not an unbiased transcriptome. Co-expression cannot establish cell lineage, projection target, cardiac function or enhancer specificity. cfos is an activity-dependent readout, not a stable driver recommendation; cx43 co-expression also requires inspection for glial/segmentation contributions. The tool allows these markers for QC but flags them. No clinical or stimulation recommendation is made.

Reproduce with python analyze.py and NumPy, using the bundled cell-level subset; python analyze.py --download checks and reprocesses the originals.
